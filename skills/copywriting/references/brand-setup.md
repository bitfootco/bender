# Brand setup

Use this reference only when no applicable `BRAND.md` exists or the user asks to create or revise one.

## Draft from evidence

Start with what the repository already establishes. Mark unknowns instead of filling them with generic branding. A useful `BRAND.md` is short enough to guide routine writing and specific enough to reject off-brand copy.

Cover only what the brand needs:

- **Audience:** primary readers, their situation, and the problem they need solved.
- **Positioning:** what the product is, who it is for, and the meaningful alternative or category.
- **Value proposition:** the main outcome and why this product can deliver it.
- **Capabilities and proof:** supported features, differentiators, examples, metrics, or claims that may be used.
- **Voice:** a few actionable traits, each with a short “do / avoid” distinction.
- **Language:** preferred terminology, naming, capitalization, and phrases to avoid.
- **Calls to action:** preferred actions and when each is appropriate.
- **Constraints:** legal, accessibility, localization, or claim restrictions.
- **Examples:** two or three short approved examples only when they clarify the rules.

## Resolve material gaps

Ask focused questions in small rounds. Prioritize decisions that change the current assignment:

1. Who must act after reading this, and what should they do?
2. What outcome matters most to them?
3. Which claims and proof are approved?
4. What should the brand sound like, and what should it never sound like?
5. Which terms and calls to action are required or prohibited?

Offer an evidence-based recommendation with each question when possible. Incorporate answers into the guide and show the revised draft. Confirmation means the user accepts it as the working source of truth; it does not freeze the document permanently.

Avoid invented personas, exhaustive tone scales, slogan workshops, competitor matrices, and long lists of adjectives unless the user's actual brand decision requires them.
